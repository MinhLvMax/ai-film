
Ultra-realistic high-end wildlife documentary imagery with authentic animal anatomy, natural behavior, realistic scale, and physically believable environments. The visual language should resemble premium BBC Earth / National Geographic-style nature cinematography, captured with professional full-frame cinema or wildlife cameras and high-quality photographic lenses.

Use **bright, clean, natural exposure** with clearly visible subjects and environmental detail. Avoid excessively dark, muddy, underexposed, or crushed-shadow imagery. Preserve realistic highlights and shadows while maintaining strong local contrast and excellent subject separation. Daytime scenes should feel naturally luminous, with soft atmospheric depth and realistic sunlight filtered through clouds, foliage, mist, dust, or humidity when appropriate.

Colors must remain natural, rich, and realistic: healthy greens, believable earth tones, accurate fur, feathers, scales, skin, water, and sky colors. Use subtle cinematic color treatment only. Avoid extreme teal-and-orange grading, oversaturation, neon colors, artificial glow, fantasy coloration, or heavily stylized filters.

Subjects must remain anatomically accurate and species-appropriate. Preserve correct body proportions, limb count, facial structure, fur, feathers, scales, markings, size, age characteristics, and natural posture. Animals must not display exaggerated human-like facial expressions, gestures, emotions, or unnatural poses.

All animal behavior must follow realistic wildlife behavior and believable physics. Movement, weight, balance, muscle response, water interaction, vegetation interaction, and environmental reactions must feel natural. Avoid floating bodies, sliding feet, unnatural acceleration, impossible jumps, rubber-like movement, excessive aggression, or theatrical behavior unless explicitly required by the shot.

Environments must feel physically real, ecologically plausible, richly detailed, and naturally integrated with the subjects. Vegetation, terrain, water, weather, atmospheric conditions, and background wildlife must match the intended habitat. Maintain realistic depth, scale, perspective, and spatial relationships.

Use cinematic but believable wildlife photography. Favor clear subject readability, strong foreground-midground-background separation, natural depth, realistic lens compression, and purposeful composition. Avoid extreme fisheye distortion, unrealistic depth of field, excessive lens flare, artificial bloom, or distracting camera effects.

Lighting direction, weather, time of day, subject appearance, environmental characteristics, relative scale, and overall color treatment must remain visually consistent across shots belonging to the same scene.

For video, motion should be smooth, physically plausible, and documentary-like. Camera movement should feel controlled and intentional: static observation, slow tracking, gentle push-ins, restrained pans or tilts, and realistic handheld movement only when appropriate. Avoid unnecessary camera spinning, rapid orbiting, aggressive zooming, excessive shake, random reframing, or artificial speed ramps.

Environmental motion should remain subtle and natural: gentle foliage movement, realistic water ripples, drifting mist, moving clouds, rainfall, dust, insects, or distant wildlife only when appropriate to the environment.

Maintain high temporal consistency in video. Subjects must not morph, change species, change markings, change body proportions, duplicate, disappear, or spontaneously alter position. Background objects, terrain, vegetation, lighting, shadows, and weather must not flicker, warp, teleport, or transform unexpectedly.

Always preserve:

* Ultra-realistic wildlife documentary realism
* Bright, clean, naturally exposed imagery
* Accurate animal anatomy and species characteristics
* Natural animal behavior
* Realistic physics and environmental interaction
* Authentic habitats and ecological context
* Natural cinematic color
* Stable lighting and visual continuity
* Professional wildlife cinematography
* High photographic detail
* Clear subject readability
* Consistent scale and spatial relationships

Avoid:

* Cartoon, illustration, anime, CGI-looking or game-like imagery
* Fantasy elements or surreal environments
* Human-like animal expressions or gestures
* Anatomical deformation or extra limbs
* Morphing, duplication, disappearing subjects, or identity changes
* Unrealistic animal behavior
* Oversaturated or neon colors
* Excessive darkness or crushed shadows
* Artificial glow, heavy bloom, or excessive lens flare
* Extreme cinematic grading
* Unrealistic depth of field
* Text, subtitles, logos, borders, or watermarks
* Unmotivated camera movement
* Lighting flicker or sudden exposure changes
* Background warping or environmental instability
